# Stumped for the Commodore 64 (plan)

**Status:** an idea for later, not started. Like the Game Boy version it's a rewrite, not a port of
`src/main.c`: the puzzles, art and music ideas carry over, the code does not. Roughly the same amount
of work as the Game Boy version.

## Why it suits the C64

- **Memory:** 64 KB is roomy for this game (the Game Boy has 8 KB of work RAM). Undo history, the level
  data and the level maker fit easily; level sets can load from disk as needed.
- **Speed:** Sokoban is turn-based, so 1 MHz is plenty for the moves, the stuck-acorn checks and the codes.
- **Sound:** the SID is the best fit of any version. Three voices, but the filter gives warm, swelling
  pads, and fast arpeggios fake full chords. Listen mode would probably sound best here.
- **Keyboard:** typing level and progress codes is easy.
- **Running it:** a `.d64` disk image runs in VICE, on SD2IEC, on the Ultimate cartridges and on a real 1541.

## Graphics (decide by mock-up)

The Game Boy look is four shades per pixel at full resolution, which the C64 can't do exactly. The
leading idea is **hi-res with coloured sprites**:

- **Hedges and grass** in hi-res character mode: two colours per 8 x 8 cell (background plus one).
  The hedges keep their bumpy outlines and leaf pattern but lose the in-between shades; choosing each
  cell's colour (darker cells for hedge edges, lighter for leaf tops) brings back much of the depth.
- **Acorns** are drawn as tiles, not sprites: the C64 shows at most 8 sprites on one line, and some
  levels have more acorns than that in a row. A sprite takes over only while an acorn is being pushed,
  or for the stuck-acorn shake.
- **The animal** as two stacked hi-res sprites (a dark outline and a coloured fill): two crisp colours at
  full resolution, close to today's animals.
- **Squirrels, the barn owl, fireflies** in the remaining sprites (sprites can be reused further down
  the screen with careful timing).

The alternatives are multicolor character mode (four colours per cell, double-wide pixels, so the
16 x 16 art is redrawn at 8 x 16) or a mix. Before writing any C64 code: render the same level all
three ways under true C64 rules (the real 16-colour palette, colours per cell, sprite limits) and pick.

- **Palettes:** the 16 colours include four greys (close to Pocket Grey), blues for Night and greens
  for a DMG-ish look: three or four palettes, not all nine.
- **Screen:** 320 x 200 with 16-pixel squares shows 20 x 12 squares, more than the Game Boy. Most
  Microban levels fit; the big ones scroll (smooth scrolling is standard C64 technique).
- **Corners:** the end caps where hedges only meet corner to corner are just more tiles.

## Other work

- **Controls:** joystick with one button, plus keys: move with the stick, fire to choose, and keys for
  undo, redo, restart, the map and the menus.
- **Music:** the generative rules (chords, key changes, acorns bringing in the beat) carry over; the
  sound engine is new, a SID player.
- **Saves:** to disk.
- **No solver** ("Solve it for me"): levels are proven by solving them, as on the Game Boy. Level codes
  and progress codes work the same as everywhere else.
- **Code:** cc65 C for the game logic, 6502 assembly for drawing, scrolling and sound. Writing the core
  (levels, moves, undo, codes, saves) once in portable C would let the Game Boy, NES and C64 versions
  share it (see [docs/NES.md](NES.md#shared-core)).
